I just released my first VST plugin! I’ve been polishing it on music jams for about an year. In this post I’ll tell some bits about euclidean drums and why I made this at all.

As a progmetal guitar-synth player, I have a crazy pedalboard with tons of effects:

OF COURSE that thing needed a drum module. Sometimes I do drums on synth jams, and I want to be able to play guitar or Exquis at the same time as switching drums and adding fills. Of course, the result is much better when you’re a dedicated drummer with full attention, but sometimes the drummer is away, or someone puts you in charge. Anyway, it’s a lot of fun to play harmonic instrument and switch drum pattern with odd number accents and also play that on your instrument.

But the problem was that my pedalboard was eating 100% of CPU with all these effects (there are some cool things, such as separate strings processing, I’ll show that later). Being a C++ programmer, I embarked on a quest to make the most performant drum sequencer + sampler ever…

But also, sometimes I did put all attention into drum parts - I explored the market of drum sequencer and found a ton of tools I didn’t like, and a couple of tools that I liked, but with imperfections.

I found a category of sequencers, which idea I find really cool:

Euclidean Sequencers#

The core idea is that you have separate tracks for each drum sample. Then, you subdivide the notes it can play into a grid. Then, you specify steps (“pulse”) and rotation:

Usually in such sequencers, you have multiple instrument tracks and they are arranged in a circular view:

The thing is that you also can change the number of steps and the speed in which it goes. That gives you an insane polyrhythm machine!

I found that thing very interesting to play with, especially when my friend played polyrhythmic arps. Any drum beat can be expressed with euclidean sequencer, and you’re able to change things up quickly without looking at the screen.

But, all existing plugins had flaws! In one, you can’t edit individual steps for some reason. The other one is ableton-only (I absolutely hate it). The third one has very cluttered UI with important functionality scattered across pages and no ability to bind MIDI quickly. And my plugin didn’t have UI…

Long story short… let’s fucking go!!! In the age of weird new technology I could just implement the UI easily and prettily, the way I think it’s best.

Permute#

It’s not flawless - drum patterns generated with euclidean polyrhythms place hits to evenly, and start to sound quite similar. You can disable steps individually, byt this is too slow. I added permute parameter that deterministically shuffles things up
